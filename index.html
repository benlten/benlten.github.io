My current research focuses on biologically inspired computer vision and perception. My work is in understanding the computational circuits that underlie robustness, adaptability and modeling.

I am a Principal Research Scientist at NAVWAR in San Diego, where I founded and direct the NeuroML and NeuroVision labs. I am completing my Ph.D. in Data Science at UC San Diego as a DoD SMART Scholar, advised by Dr. Virginia de Sa in Cognitive Science and Prof. Gary Cottrell in CSE. Previously I received my M.S. in Computer Science from Georgia Tech, where I was advised by Prof. David Joyner and Prof. Ashok Goel, and my B.S. in Electrical Engineering from the University of Rochester.

Benjamin TenWolde, Virginia de Sa

Conference on Cognitive Computational Neuroscience (CCN) · 2026

Two widely used memorability predictors track pixel-level colour and frequency statistics rather than the people and scenes humans actually remember — imperceptible perturbations move their scores far more than the presence of a person does.

    Cosine is Human: The Reproducibility Ceiling of Perceptual Similarity
    B. TenWolde · NeurIPS 2026, Evaluations and Datasets Track

    Eleven perceptual-similarity metrics evaluated on the full NIGHTS corpus rather than its unanimous-agreement subset. Untrained cosine distance on a frozen self-supervised backbone reaches 74.1%, while a split-half estimate shows the majority labels themselves reproduce only 70.1% of the time — the extra accuracy from supervised training reflects matching the collected votes, not closer agreement with human judgment.
